# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective restaurant partners — independent owners and operators evaluating whether Rasvia is the right digital partner. They browse on phone or laptop, often between services or after hours, comparing options quickly. They need to understand credibility, fit with their existing POS (Toast, Clover, Square), and whether Rasvia respects their margins. Secondary audiences (floor staff, guests on join flows) exist but marketing pages optimize for the partner decision first.

## Product Purpose

RasviaWeb is the public face of Rasvia: a restaurant management platform combining custom-branded mobile apps, web storefronts, waitlists, tableside ordering, kitchen display, and partner dashboards. The marketing site exists to convert independent restaurants into partners by demonstrating operator-grade tools—not generic templates. Success looks like a prospect understanding what Rasvia does in under a minute, trusting it as serious restaurant tech, and taking the next step (contact, demo, signup).

## Brand Personality

Confident operator — direct, no fluff, built for people who run restaurants. Voice speaks owner-to-owner: margins, POS integration, and service realities matter more than startup adjectives. Premium enough to justify a monthly fee, but never precious or performative.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: warm cream/beige body backgrounds, small uppercase eyebrow kickers above every section, identical icon + heading + text card grids
- AI-scaffolded "modern" marketing: gradient hero metrics, decorative glass cards, numbered section markers (01 / 02 / 03) without real sequence
- Template POS marketing: stock dining photos, buzzword-heavy copy, undifferentiated feature walls that could belong to any ordering app

## Design Principles

1. **Operator credibility first** — Feel like restaurant tech owners already trust (Square/Toast-adjacent clarity), not a YC demo day slide deck.
2. **Plain speech about real constraints** — Lead with margins, POS fit, and setup time; avoid abstract "digital transformation" language.
3. **Show the product, not decoration** — Marketing mockups and screenshots should reveal actual workflows; ornament that doesn't teach is waste.
4. **Distinct without exotic** — Stand apart from generic SaaS scaffolding while keeping the scannable hierarchy busy owners expect.
5. **One coherent brand across surfaces** — Marketing, product pages, and partner dashboard chrome should feel like the same company; prospects should recognize Rasvia when they log in.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA for marketing pages: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, keyboard-accessible navigation and CTAs, visible focus states, and `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for any motion beyond essential feedback.
